Surveyor 3

MOON

What Was Surveyor 3?

Surveyor 3 was an unmanned robotic spacecraft developed by NASA as part of its Surveyor programme. It was sent to the Moon to demonstrate that a spacecraft could make a successful soft landing and study the lunar surface. It was the second Surveyor spacecraft to achieve a successful soft landing on the Moon.

Mission and Purpose

Surveyor 3 was launched on April 17, 1967, and landed on the Moon on April 20, 1967, in the southeastern region of Oceanus Procellarum. It carried a television camera and a mechanical surface sampler to examine the lunar environment.

Its main purpose was to study the Moon's soil and determine whether the surface could support the weight of an Apollo Lunar Module. The mechanical sampler could dig trenches, move lunar material and perform tests on the surface.

Discoveries and Scientific Contributions

Surveyor 3 transmitted 6,326 television pictures of the lunar surface. Its mechanical sampler performed digging, bearing and impact tests to examine the properties of lunar soil.

The experiments showed that the lunar soil had a consistency similar to wet sand and was strong enough to support an Apollo Lunar Module. This information helped scientists understand the Moon's surface and prepare for future human exploration.

What Happened to Surveyor 3?

Surveyor 3 continued transmitting information until May 4, 1967, when contact with the spacecraft was lost after the lunar night began.

More than two years later, Apollo 12 astronauts Charles Conrad Jr. and Alan L. Bean landed approximately 180 metres from Surveyor 3 on November 18, 1969.

During their second spacewalk on November 19, they visited the spacecraft and removed several components, including its television camera and soil scoop. These parts were brought back to Earth so scientists could study the effects of their long exposure to the lunar environment. Surveyor 3 remains on the Moon today.
